Deliverable 2 / 5 · Mood Board — Art Direction

The objects, in
charcoal and grain.

This web doc is about two things left on a stage — cardboard boxes and a wooden chair. The look has to feel touched, not rendered: raw, weighted, made by hand. No slick vectors, no flat fills. Charcoal, graphite, raking light, and macro photographs of real material under stress.

A · The aesthetic stance

What we reject, what we reach for.

✕ Not this

  • Slick, vector-perfect illustration
  • Flat fills and clean geometric icons
  • Even, ambient studio lighting
  • Glossy 3D renders of objects
  • Stock photography, drop-shadow gloss
  • Anything that looks generated by a template

◆ This

  • Raw charcoal sketches — visible hand, broken line
  • Graphite texture — smudge, tooth, paper grain
  • High-contrast spotlight shadows — one hard source
  • Macro photographs of cardboard and wood under load
  • Friction and weight you can almost feel
  • Sketchbook plates pinned to a dark stage
B · Charcoal object studies

Draw them before you photograph them.

The two protagonists, rendered as loose charcoal studies on newsprint — broken, doubled lines, never closed vector paths. These set the drawing language for section dividers and chapter openers.

C · Macro material plates

Get close enough to feel friction.

Full-bleed macro photographs of the real objects, lit by one raking source so every fibre, crease, and scuff throws a shadow. These are shot-direction plates — what to capture, and how.

D · Light & shadow

One hard source, long cast shadow.

The objects live in a darkened house. A single raking spotlight carves them out and throws a long shadow across the floor — high contrast, theatrical, never evenly lit.

Charcoal, paper, kraft, wood.

F · Typography

Extreme legibility, an editorial-notebook voice.

The pairing has to do two things at once: read effortlessly for every visitor, and feel like an artist's working notebook. Four faces, each with one job.

The voices, together

A body that reveals itself

— she barely moves, and it's everything

Running text is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers: open shapes, unmistakable letters, nothing ambiguous. It carries every word a visitor actually needs to read.

macro_03_fold_crease.tif · f/8 · raking key

The pairing
  • Editorial displayFraunces — a high-contrast, slightly wonky serif. Headlines, pull-quotes, the literary voice.
  • Accessible bodyAtkinson Hyperlegible — all running text. Extreme legibility is the baseline, not an add-on.
  • Notebook annotationCaveat — a handwritten margin hand for notes, arrows, and the sketchbook feel.
  • Technical captionIBM Plex Mono — typewriter labels: shot notes, file codes, timecodes.

The rule that keeps it accessible: the handwritten face is decorative only — never navigation, never essential content. Anything a visitor must read is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible.

G · How the pages compose

Sketchbook plates pinned to a dark stage.

The layout logic, so every page feels like the same hand assembled it.

01

The stage is the page

A deep, grained linen-charcoal field with generous negative space. Content arrives in pools of light; the dark between is left to breathe.

02

Plates, slightly askew

Charcoal studies and material photos sit on newsprint cards, faintly rotated, with tape and soft shadow — placed by hand, never grid-snapped.

03

Macro as the chapter opener

Each chapter opens full-bleed on one macro photograph — fibre, grain, or crease — before any text. Material first, words second.

04

Charcoal as the divider

A loose object study marks each section break, drawn not rendered, so the seams of the site show the hand.

05

Annotations in the margin

Handwritten notes and arrows live in the margins — the artist thinking aloud — while the column stays clean and readable.

06

One light, long shadow

Emphasis comes from a single raking source and a long cast shadow, not from colour or glow. Contrast does the work.
