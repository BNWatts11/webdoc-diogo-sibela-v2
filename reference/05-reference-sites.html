Four projects that already
solved these problems.

Three you named, plus one I'm adding because it is the closest precedent for turning movement qualities into something a visitor can read. Each card states what the project is, why it fits this web doc, and the one mechanic worth taking.

The references

What to study, and what to take from each.

REF 01 One Shared House Anton & Irene
2016 · self-funded onesharedhouse.com

A ten-minute film about a communal house in Amsterdam. Optional interactive panels sit at the foot of the screen — visitors who want more depth open them without losing their place; visitors who just want to watch, watch. It closes with a survey whose results aggregate from viewers around the world.

Take for this project

A calm linear spine with opt-in depth, plus an end mechanic where the audience leaves a trace — which feeds straight into the collective “Final Map” of audience box-compositions in the Post-Dance section.

REF 02 Welcome to Pine Point The Goggles
NFB Canada · 2011
Flash-era archive pinepoint.nfb.ca

A vanished mining town told as a scrapbook. You drag and rearrange ephemera as if it were your own archive; a chaptered spine with always-visible navigation keeps you oriented; sound runs through every page. Every element is custom — nothing reads as a default web template.

Take for this project

Objects as navigation. Diogo's boxes are the literal version — draggable, stackable elements that move the visitor through the Post-Dance section. Plus the persistent chapter menu that keeps everyone found.

REF 03 Hollow Elaine McMillion Sheldon
2013 · Peabody winner hollowdocumentary.com

A participatory portrait of McDowell County, West Virginia, where residents recorded their own video portraits rather than being filmed about. The place is built from many first-person voices stitched into a scrollable map of a community.

Take for this project

The participatory ethic — subjects speak for themselves. This is the model for handing narration to Isabela and Diogo instead of explaining them over b-roll. It is the spine of your whole brief: move the camera off the choreographer.

+ Added by me REF 04 Synchronous Objects W. Forsythe + Ohio State
2009 synchronousobjects.osu.edu

An online companion to the dance One Flat Thing, reproduced that translates choreographic structure into interactive visual scores — cueing, alignment, and movement patterns you can read as data. The closest precedent for representing how a body moves, not just that it moved.

Take for this project

The grammar for the timeline's movement-quality lanes — Isabela's breath and suspension, Diogo's weight and instability — drawn as readable visual tracks, never as motion-capture spectacle. It keeps the technology invisible, the way Tânia Carvalho asks.

What they share

Four lessons, one direction.

Read together, the references point the same way — and it matches the brief.

01

One spine, opt-in depth

All keep a clear path through, with extra layers a visitor chooses — never a maze. (One Shared House · Pine Point)

02

Let the subjects speak

The strongest ones hand the microphone to the people in the story. (Hollow)

03

Objects carry meaning

Physical things — ephemera, a body's structure — become the interface, not decoration. (Pine Point · Synchronous Objects)

04

Custom, quiet, sound-led

None look like a template. Sound and restraint do the work — exactly Carvalho's aesthetic.
